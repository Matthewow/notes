### gun for

**Meaning:** 努力争取或以某个目标为方向。

**Examples**

- I think we’re still gunning for a September 22 internal launch date to avoid thrash, give us a high-confidence date for the external launch, and avoid further churn in downstream timelines.
- The team is gunning for a substantial reduction in API latency before the end of the quarter.

**Note:** This is informal and can sound forceful. *Aim for* or *target* is more neutral.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-08-thrash -->
### thrash

**Meaning:** 因为方向不明确或决定反复变化而产生的无效工作和返工。

**Examples**

- We chose an internal launch date early to avoid thrash and give dependent teams enough time to plan.
- The unclear requirements created a lot of thrash because engineers repeatedly rewrote the same workflow.

**Note:** In engineering, *thrash* can also describe a computer repeatedly swapping memory or retrying work without making useful progress.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-08-churn -->
### churn

**Meaning:** 持续或反复的变化，导致计划、需求或时间安排不稳定。

**Examples**

- A stable internal deadline will help us avoid further churn in downstream timelines.
- Frequent changes to the API contract created unnecessary churn for every team that depended on it.

**Note:** *Customer churn* has a different specific meaning: customers stopping their use of a product or service.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-08-explicit-action-item -->
### explicit action item

**Meaning:** 明确提出并记录的待办任务。

**Examples**

- Is there anything we didn’t talk about today that we should have, or any explicit action items that we should add here? None come to mind for me.
- Before we end the meeting, let’s make every explicit action item clear by assigning an owner and a due date.

**Note:** An *action item* is a task that someone should complete after a discussion. *Explicit* emphasizes that the task is clearly stated rather than only implied.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-08-throw-someone-a-curveball -->
### throw someone a curveball

**Meaning:** 突然给某人一个意料之外的问题、变化或挑战。

**Examples**

- When I threw the interviewee a simple curveball, they panicked and initially wrote the wrong solution.
- The client threw us a curveball by requesting a different authentication method two days before launch.

**Note:** A *curveball* is unexpected. It does not have to be difficult; a simple variation can still reveal how someone responds to change.
