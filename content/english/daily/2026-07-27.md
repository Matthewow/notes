### top contender

**Meaning:** 最有希望的人选、方案或候选者之一。

**Examples**

- This design is the top contender because it performed best in user testing.
- She is a top contender for the engineering manager role.

**Note:** Use *the top contender* for the current leading option. Use *a top contender* for one of several strong options.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-bring-in-a-new-segment-of-users -->
### bring in a new segment of users

**Meaning:** 吸引或引入一个新的用户群体。

**Examples**

- This feature could bring in a new segment of users who currently rely on traditional payment methods.
- Supporting smaller businesses may bring in a new segment of users without changing the core product.

**Note:** *Bring in* is more natural than *bring* here.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-backlash -->
### backlash

**Meaning:** 对某项决定、变化或公开事件产生的强烈负面反应。

**Examples**

- There has been a bit of backlash in the market since the company announced the pricing change.
- The team expected some backlash from existing users, so they prepared a clear explanation before the launch.

**Note:** Say *a bit of backlash*, not *a bit backlash*. *Backlash* is normally uncountable.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-mark-something-as-a-no-go -->
### mark something as a no-go

**Meaning:** 将某个方案标记为不可行、不可接受或不能继续推进。

**Examples**

- We marked the proposal as a no-go because it would require storing sensitive customer data.
- After reviewing the legal restrictions, the team marked international expansion as a no-go for this quarter.

**Note:** *No-go* is informal workplace language. *Not feasible* is a more formal alternative.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-sprinkle-something-throughout -->
### sprinkle something throughout

**Meaning:** 将少量内容分散地加入整个流程、文档或体验中。

**Examples**

- We can sprinkle educational content throughout the user journey so that people feel safer at each step.
- She sprinkled practical examples throughout the presentation to make the technical concepts easier to understand.

**Note:** In the original sentence, lowercase *education* is grammatically possible, but *educational content* is more natural and specific.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-for-brevity -->
### for brevity

**Meaning:** 为了简洁；为了避免提供不必要的细节。

**Examples**

- For brevity, I left the implementation details out of the meeting notes.
- The error messages use shortened field names for brevity, but the full names appear in the documentation.

**Note:** This is common in written or formal communication. In conversation, *to keep it short* is more casual.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-pilot-program -->
### pilot program

**Meaning:** 在全面推出之前，用较小范围进行测试的试点项目。

**Examples**

- I’m helping organize the pilot programs, so I have some context about how the participants were selected.
- We will run a pilot program with two teams before making the workflow available to the whole company.

**Note:** *The pilots* probably means pilot programs or small-scale trials in this context. If it refers to specific pilot sessions, use *pilot sessions* instead.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-chime-in -->
### chime in

**Meaning:** 加入讨论并发表意见，通常是在自己有相关信息或经验时。

**Examples**

- I’m helping organize the pilot programs, so I thought I could chime in with some additional context.
- Feel free to chime in if you notice a risk that the rest of us have missed.

**Note:** This is friendly, informal workplace language.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-artifact -->
### artifact

**Meaning:** 在项目中产生并供团队参考的材料或成果，例如文档、设计稿、图表或原型。

**Examples**

- Are there any artifacts, such as design documents or prototypes, that we can review at this point?
- The team keeps important project artifacts in one shared folder so that new members can quickly understand past decisions.

**Note:** In product and software work, *artifact* means a work product or project output, not only a historical object. The original question can be simplified to: “Are there any artifacts or documents we can look at at this point?”

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-07-27-steer-someone-away-from-something -->
### steer someone away from something

**Meaning:** 引导某人避开某个选择、方法或行动方向。

**Examples**

- I think I was trying to steer them away from options that would create additional compliance risks.
- The onboarding flow steers users away from advanced settings until they understand the basic workflow.

**Note:** The usual structure is *steer someone away from something*.
