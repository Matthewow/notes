### closing remarks

**Meaning:** 结束语；在会议、演讲或活动结束前所说的最后几句话。

**Examples**

- Before we end the meeting, does anyone have any closing remarks or important concerns that we have not discussed?
- In her closing remarks, the director thanked everyone who helped with the product launch.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-07-majorly-problematic -->
### majorly problematic

**Meaning:** 存在很严重的问题；会造成重大麻烦。

**Examples**

- So far, I don’t see anything majorly problematic with the proposed implementation.
- The delay is inconvenient, but it is not majorly problematic because we still have enough time before the release.

**Note:** *Majorly* is informal. In professional writing, *seriously problematic* or *a major problem* is usually more natural.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-07-contextually -->
### contextually

**Meaning:** 从具体语境、背景或当前情况来看。

**Examples**

- Contextually, the payment method makes sense for large purchases, but it may confuse users during smaller transactions.
- The error message is grammatically correct, but contextually it does not explain what the user should do next.

**Note:** *Contextually* focuses on how something fits its surrounding situation. *In context* is a more common alternative in everyday speech.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-07-get-some-brainpower-on-something -->
### get some brainpower on something

**Meaning:** 让一些人参与思考某个问题，以获得更多想法或找到解决方案。

**Examples**

- Let’s share the problem with the wider engineering team so we can get some additional brainpower on it.
- If you have any thoughts, put them in the document so we can get more brainpower on the problem before making a decision.

**Note:** This is informal. *Get more eyes on something* focuses more on review, while *get some brainpower on something* focuses on thinking and problem-solving.

<!-- english-item-id: 2026-09-07-noodle-on-something -->
### noodle on something

**Meaning:** 花些时间非正式地思考或琢磨某件事。

**Examples**

- Let me noodle on this for a while and figure out the best path forward before our next meeting.
- I’ll noodle on the API design this afternoon and share a few options with the team tomorrow.

**Note:** This is informal North American English. *Think it over* or *give it some thought* is more neutral.
